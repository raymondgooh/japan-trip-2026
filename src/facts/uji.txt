Byodo-in's Phoenix Hall is printed on the Japanese 10-yen coin, a design in use since 1951.
The phoenix on the roof of Byodo-in appeared on the back of the old 10,000-yen note.
Byodo-in was a villa of the powerful statesman Fujiwara no Michinaga. His son turned it into a temple in 1052.
The Phoenix Hall was built in 1053 and has survived for nearly 1,000 years.
Byodo-in means "Temple of Equality".
The Phoenix Hall gets its name from its shape, like a phoenix spreading its wings, and from the two bronze phoenixes on its roof.
The Phoenix Hall was designed to recreate Amida Buddha's Pure Land paradise on earth, reflected in the pond in front.
Inside the Phoenix Hall is a gilded Amida Buddha by the master sculptor Jocho.
The walls of the Phoenix Hall hold 52 small wooden Bodhisattvas riding on clouds, many playing musical instruments.
The original bronze phoenixes and the temple bell are kept in Byodo-in's Hoshokan museum.
Byodo-in's bell is one of the three most famous temple bells in Japan, known for its beauty.
The Phoenix Hall was restored in 2012–2014 to its original red colour.
Ujigami Shrine's main hall, from around 1060, is thought to be the oldest surviving Shinto shrine building in Japan.
Both Byodo-in and Ujigami Shrine are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
The last ten chapters of The Tale of Genji, the "Uji chapters", are set in Uji.
The Tale of Genji, written around 1008 by Murasaki Shikibu, is often called the world's first novel.
Uji has a Tale of Genji Museum.
A statue of Murasaki Shikibu sits near Uji Bridge.
Uji Bridge is one of Japan's oldest bridges, first built in 646 according to tradition.
Uji Bridge has a small balcony on its upstream side. Toyotomi Hideyoshi is said to have drawn water there for his tea ceremonies.
Tsuen, the tea shop at the east end of Uji Bridge, has been run by the same family since 1160.
The Uji River flows out of Lake Biwa, Japan's largest lake.
Battles of the Genpei War were fought at Uji Bridge in 1180 and 1184.
Uji was a favourite country retreat for Heian-era nobles.
Tea first arrived in Japan in the 9th century with monks returning from China.
The monk Eisai's 1211 book "Kissa Yojoki" promoted tea as good for health.
Shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu loved Uji tea and named seven special tea gardens in Uji. Only Okunoyama is still farmed.
Uji tea farmers developed the method of shading tea plants before harvest. This boosts flavour and makes the leaves a deep green.
Matcha is made by grinding shaded tencha leaves in stone mills, which produce only about 30–40 g an hour.
Tencha leaves are steamed and dried, then the stems and veins are removed before the leaves are ground into matcha.
Gyokuro, one of Japan's finest green teas, was developed in the Uji area in the 1830s.
Hojicha, roasted green tea, is said to have been first made in Kyoto in the 1920s.
In the Edo period, tea from Uji was carried to the shogun in Edo every year in a grand procession called the "Ochatsubo Dochu".
The children's song "Zui Zui Zukkorobashi" is said to be about hiding from the shogun's tea-jar procession.
"Uji tea" covers tea grown in Kyoto, Nara, Shiga and Mie prefectures, as long as it's processed in Kyoto.
Wazuka, a small town in southern Kyoto, grows a large share of Uji tea on hillside terraces.
Matcha became so popular worldwide in 2024–2025 that many Uji shops limited how much each customer could buy.
A matcha whisk (chasen) is carved from a single piece of bamboo and often has around 80 thin tines.
Nakamura Tokichi, founded in 1854, is one of Uji's best-known tea houses and has long queues for its matcha parfaits.
Taiho-an, a teahouse run by Uji city, lets visitors try a simple tea ceremony for a small fee.
Uji makes chasoba, buckwheat noodles mixed with green tea.
Ujigami Shrine's Kirihara spring is the only one left of Uji's "seven famous springs".
Uji Shrine's "mikaeri usagi" rabbit, looking back, is a symbol of the prince the shrine honours, who was guided by a rabbit.
Ujigami Shrine sells little rabbit-shaped fortunes.
Mampuku-ji in Uji was founded in 1661 by Ingen, a monk from China, and is built in Ming Chinese style.
Ingen is credited with bringing green beans to Japan, which is why they're called "ingen-mame".
The big wooden fish hanging at Mampuku-ji is struck to call monks to meals. It's the ancestor of the "mokugyo" drum used in Buddhist chanting.
Mampuku-ji serves "fucha ryori", Chinese-style vegetarian temple food.
Koshoji temple's Kotozaka slope is lined with maples that turn red in late November.
Mimurotoji temple in Uji is famous for its 20,000 hydrangeas in June.
Mt Kisen in Uji is named after Kisen Hoshi, one of Japan's "Six Poetic Immortals".
Uji holds cormorant fishing on the river in summer, and some of its cormorant masters are women.
In 2014, Uji became the first place to hatch and raise cormorants in captivity for traditional fishing.
The Agata Matsuri in June is a "dark festival" held at night with the lights turned off.
Uji holds a tea festival every October in honour of tea masters like Sen no Rikyu.
Uji is the setting of the anime "Sound! Euphonium", and fans visit spots from the show.
Kyoto Animation, one of Japan's best-known anime studios, is based in Uji.
A post box near JR Uji Station is shaped like a tea jar.
Uji is only about 20–30 minutes by train from central Kyoto.
Uji's town logo and many of its manhole covers feature tea leaves.
