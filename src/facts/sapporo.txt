Sapporo was laid out as a planned city from 1869, which is why its streets form a neat grid.
Sapporo's name comes from Ainu words often explained as "dry, great river".
Sapporo addresses work like coordinates, such as "North 3, West 4", counted from Odori Park and the Sosei River.
Sapporo is Japan's fifth-largest city, with about 2 million people.
Sapporo gets around 5 m of snow a year, a huge amount for a city of its size.
Sapporo hosted the 1972 Winter Olympics, the first Winter Games held in Asia.
The Okurayama ski jump, used at the 1972 Olympics, has a viewing deck at the top where you can see the drop.
Sapporo Snow Festival began in 1950, when local high school students built six snow statues in Odori Park.
Japan's Self-Defense Forces help build the giant snow sculptures for the Snow Festival.
Odori Park is a long green strip about 1.5 km long that divides Sapporo into north and south.
The Sapporo White Illumination, which lights up Odori Park from late November, was Japan's first big winter light-up event (1981).
The Munich Christmas Market fills Odori Park from late November. Munich is Sapporo's sister city.
Sapporo TV Tower, at the east end of Odori Park, is 147 m tall and has a giant digital clock.
Sapporo TV Tower's mascot is "Terebi-tou-san", a tower with a face (the name means Mr TV Father).
The Sapporo Clock Tower (1878) is one of Japan's oldest clock towers. It's often jokingly called one of Japan's most disappointing sights because it's so small.
The Clock Tower's clock was made by E. Howard & Co. of Boston and still chimes on the hour.
The Clock Tower was originally the drill hall of Sapporo Agricultural College.
American educator William S. Clark helped found Sapporo Agricultural College (now Hokkaido University) in 1876.
Clark's parting words to his students, "Boys, be ambitious!", are still famous in Japan.
The statue of Clark at Hitsujigaoka Observation Hill points into the distance.
Sapporo Beer, founded in 1876, is Japan's oldest beer brand.
The red star on Sapporo Beer is the North Star symbol of the old Hokkaido Development Commission.
The Sapporo Beer Museum is in a red-brick building that was once a sugar factory.
Sapporo Classic beer is sold only in Hokkaido.
Jingisukan (Genghis Khan) is Sapporo's signature dish: lamb grilled on a dome-shaped skillet said to look like a warrior's helmet.
Lamb became popular in Hokkaido because sheep were raised there in the early 1900s for wool.
Sapporo is the birthplace of miso ramen, created in the 1950s–60s.
Sapporo-style ramen often has a layer of melted lard on top to keep the soup hot in freezing weather.
Sapporo ramen usually uses thick, curly, chewy noodles.
Ganso Ramen Yokocho in Susukino is a narrow alley packed with about 17 tiny ramen shops.
Soup curry, a spicy, runny curry with big chunks of vegetables, was born in Sapporo in the 1970s.
"Shime parfait" is a Sapporo habit: ending a night out with an ice-cream parfait instead of ramen.
Zangi, Hokkaido-style fried chicken, is marinated more strongly than regular karaage.
Susukino, where you're staying, is the biggest nightlife district north of Tokyo, with thousands of bars and restaurants.
The Nikka Whisky billboard with the bearded "King of Blenders" is one of Susukino's best-known landmarks.
Tanukikoji is a covered shopping arcade about 900 m long, with roots going back to 1873.
Tanukikoji means "raccoon dog alley". One story says it was named after the women there who "bewitched" men like shape-shifting tanuki.
Nijo Market, near Susukino, is the place for seafood rice bowls (kaisendon) piled with crab, salmon roe and sea urchin.
Hokkaido is famous for three big crabs: hairy crab (kegani), king crab and snow crab.
Chi-Ka-Ho, an underground walkway, connects Sapporo Station to Odori, and with Pole Town you can walk about 2 km to Susukino without going outside.
Sapporo's subway trains run on rubber tyres, which makes them quieter.
Sapporo's streetcar line became a loop in 2015.
In winter, Sapporo's "sasara densha" trams use giant bamboo brushes to sweep snow off the tracks, a method used since the 1920s.
Sapporo spends around 20 billion yen a year clearing snow.
Some Sapporo pavements and roads are heated to stop snow and ice building up.
Sapporo puts boxes of free grit at crossings in winter so you can scatter it where it's slippery.
Locals walk on ice in short, flat-footed "penguin steps". Shops sell clip-on shoe spikes.
In early December, the sun sets in Sapporo at around 4 pm.
Average December temperatures in Sapporo are around freezing, and it's often colder at night.
Mt Moiwa's night view over Sapporo was chosen as one of Japan's "New Three Major Night Views".
The JR Tower observation deck at Sapporo Station is 160 m up, on the 38th floor.
Shiroi Koibito, white-chocolate cookies, are made in Sapporo, and the factory has a fun theme park.
Royce', famous for silky nama chocolate, is a Hokkaido company.
Snow Brand (Yukijirushi) Milk started in Sapporo.
Hokkaido produces about half of Japan's milk.
Hokkaido grows most of Japan's potatoes.
A pair of Yubari melons from Hokkaido once sold at auction for 5 million yen.
Hokkaido's Seicomart convenience stores often top customer satisfaction rankings and cook fresh food in-store.
Yakisoba Bento instant noodles are sold almost only in Hokkaido. You use the water you drain off to make a soup.
Crypton Future Media, the company behind Hatsune Miku, is based in Sapporo, and "Snow Miku" is the Snow Festival's mascot.
Hokkaido was called Ezo until 1869, when it was renamed and became part of Japan's government system.
The Ainu are the indigenous people of Hokkaido. Japan formally recognised them as indigenous by law in 2019.
Many Hokkaido place names, including Sapporo and Otaru, come from Ainu words.
Hokkaido is Japan's largest prefecture, with about a fifth of the country's land but only about 4% of its people.
Hokkaido has no proper rainy season, unlike most of Japan.
The Seikan Tunnel under the sea links Hokkaido to Honshu and is about 54 km long.
Hokkaido brown bears are much bigger than the black bears on Japan's main island, and sightings near Sapporo's suburbs hit record levels in 2025.
Wild Ezo red foxes live in Hokkaido. They're cute, but don't touch or feed them because they can carry parasites.
The shima-enaga, a tiny round white bird nicknamed the "snow fairy", is one of Hokkaido's most popular animals.
Hokkaido Shrine, in Maruyama Park, enshrines the gods of Hokkaido's settlement and Emperor Meiji.
Moerenuma Park, designed by artist Isamu Noguchi, was built on top of a former rubbish landfill.
Sapporo Dome can slide a whole grass football pitch in and out of the stadium.
The Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters baseball team moved to Es Con Field in 2023, which has a hot spring and sauna overlooking the field.
Jozankei Onsen, about an hour from Sapporo, has a kappa (river imp) as its mascot.
Sapporo's population passed 1 million in 1970.
Sapporo's early planners brought in American advisers, which is why its layout and dairy farming have an American feel.
The Sapporo Agricultural College campus became Hokkaido University, and its grounds are open to visitors.
Hokkaido milk soft-serve ice cream is popular even in the middle of winter.
