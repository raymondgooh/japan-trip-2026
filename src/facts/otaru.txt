Otaru's name comes from an Ainu word for a river flowing through sandy ground.
Otaru Canal was completed in 1923 and is about 1.1 km long.
Otaru Canal was made by filling in land along the shore rather than by digging, which is why it curves gently.
In the 1980s, half of Otaru Canal was filled in for a road. Local residents campaigned to save the rest.
Otaru Canal has about 63 gas lamps, which are lit at dusk.
The old stone warehouses along the canal are now restaurants, shops and a beer hall.
Otaru was once Hokkaido's financial centre and was nicknamed "the Wall Street of the North".
Otaru grew rich as a port for herring fishing and coal shipping.
Herring barons built huge "herring mansions" (nishin goten) along the coast near Otaru.
The former Bank of Japan Otaru branch (1912) was designed by Tatsuno Kingo, the architect of Tokyo Station. It's now a free museum.
Otaru's glass industry started in the Meiji period by making oil lamps and glass fishing floats.
Kitaichi Glass began in 1901 making oil lamps and fishing floats.
Kitaichi Hall is lit only by 167 oil lamps.
Sakaimachi Street is Otaru's main shopping street for glass, music boxes and sweets.
The Otaru Music Box Museum's main hall, from 1912, is packed with tens of thousands of music boxes.
The steam clock outside the Otaru Music Box Museum was made by Raymond Saunders, the Vancouver clockmaker behind Gastown's famous steam clock, and it whistles every 15 minutes.
LeTAO's "Double Fromage" cheesecake is Otaru's most famous sweet.
Otaru is known for its sushi, and has a street lined with sushi restaurants.
Naruto in Otaru serves "half chicken", half a chicken deep-fried whole.
Ankake yakisoba, fried noodles topped with thick gravy, is Otaru's local comfort food.
Panju, small pancake sweets filled with red bean, are a classic Otaru snack.
Otaru Beer is brewed using German methods under the strict Reinheitsgebot purity law.
Hokkaido's first railway, opened in 1880, ran between Otaru and Sapporo. Part of the old Temiya Line is now a walking path.
On the train from Sapporo to Otaru, sit on the right side for views of the sea.
Otaru is about 30–45 minutes from Sapporo by JR train.
Otaru is a key setting for Shunji Iwai's 1995 film "Love Letter", which is very popular in Asia.
The Otaru Snow Light Path festival in February fills the canal and streets with candles in snow lanterns.
The Mt Tengu ropeway has views over Otaru and the bay, and there's a hall full of long-nosed tengu masks at the top.
The Nikka Whisky Yoichi Distillery, founded by Masataka Taketsuru, is about 25 minutes from Otaru.
Taketsuru and his Scottish wife Rita inspired the popular TV drama "Massan".
Shakotan, near Otaru, is famous for sea urchin (uni) in summer.
Otaru's population peaked at around 200,000 in the 1960s. Today it's about 110,000.
Otaru Suitengu Shrine sits on a hill with views over the port.
Otaru has many Western-style stone buildings from its banking boom.
Many of Otaru's old warehouses were built of stone partly to protect goods from fire.
Hand-blown glass workshops in Otaru let visitors try glassblowing or make glass beads.
Otaru's music box shops will let you choose the tune for a custom music box.
The Kitaichi Venetian Art Museum has a full-size gondola and Venetian glass.
Otaru's canal is especially pretty at dusk, when the gas lamps come on and the warehouses are lit up.
